# 7. Generate platform covers

Read `SKILL.md`, the project’s `palette.json`, the active cover template (snapshot `templates/covers/platform-cover-v1.json` for a new project), and the user’s title/artist/series text. Generate covers after palette/setup, independently of lyric-card approval.

Use the supplied 1:1 artwork as the artwork source. Use the established series layout: left 44.5% artwork plus right light-paper information field for landscape; top 40% artwork plus lower light-paper information field for portrait. In landscape, the full-height vertical accent divider sits at the artwork boundary, text starts in a narrow 1%-width gutter after its right edge, the long header rule starts flush at that right edge around 9.7% canvas height, and a short 21%-canvas-width underline sits directly below the two benefit rows. It is not a bottom-edge rule. Portrait uses a horizontal artwork divider and bottom-edge rule. Draw oversized dark title text, the artist, and—when supplied—a short accent-colour subtitle immediately below the artist (for example, `P3R ED`). Then draw exactly two benefit rows: `歌词同步高亮 · 假名注释` then `罗马音 · 逐词拆解`. In portrait, dynamically compact the two-row stack so its final glyph stays above the bottom rule; fail rather than overlap it. Use the same verified bold CJK font and palette magic colors as the video. Do not place controls, lyric cards, a spectrum, dark-gradient panels, bullets, or text outlines on a cover.

Write `project/render/cover-content.json` with `slug`, `title`, `artist`, optional `subtitle`/`series`/four `benefits`, and requested `{name,width,height,artworkFocus?}` outputs. `artworkFocus` is `left`, `center` (default), or `right`; use it when a cover’s subject would otherwise be cropped. Then run `python SKILL_ROOT/scripts/render_cover.py --cover COVER --palette PALETTE --config COVER_CONFIG --output-dir FINAL_DIR --run-id ID --report QA_REPORT`, substituting absolute paths. It measures every title/artist/subtitle/benefit bound, fails on overflow, and writes all requested `16x9`, `4x3`, `9x16`, or `3x4` exports through the same normalized layout. Mobile readability overrides conventional safety margins, but no glyph may leave the canvas.
